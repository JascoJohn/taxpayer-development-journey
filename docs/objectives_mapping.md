# The five core objectives and international frameworks (Editing Pass 1b, R5)

Read-only desk research done on 2026-11-13 (web lookups only; no change to the objectives). The objectives, in the report's own wording and order, are the five rows of every stage's "Link to Core Objectives" table: **Expanding the Tax Base; Enhancing Digital Tax Services; Simplifying Compliance for SMEs; Improving Dispute Resolution; Reducing Collection Costs.**

**Result: the mapping is clear for three objectives and weak (partly unverified) for two, so the introduction presents the objectives as "the framework's design objectives, chosen for their relevance to developing-country tax administration". It does NOT say they are "widely recognised".** Page numbers below are the *printed* page numbers of the document (PDF page = printed page + 2 for the TADAT Field Guide).

Main source (read in full text, locally extracted): **TADAT Secretariat, *Tax Administration Diagnostic Assessment Tool (TADAT) Field Guide*, November 2015** (IMF-hosted TADAT programme; nine Performance Outcome Areas, 28 indicators). **Not obtained (so not used):** the OECD *Tax Administration* series (cost-of-collection ratio, digital transformation chapter) and the OECD working paper *The Design of Presumptive Tax Regimes* (https://oecd.org/tax/the-design-of-presumptive-tax-regimes-141239bb-en.htm): the OECD site refused automated access (HTTP 403), so no section or page could be verified; and the ATAF/ICTD policy paper on taxing informal economies (https://www.ictd.ac/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/EN-ICTD-Policy_-Paper.pdf) came back as unreadable PDF data. These are plausible further sources for objectives 2, 3 and 5 but nothing from them is relied on.

**Consequence for the report.** Objectives 1, 2 and 4 correspond directly to TADAT performance areas; objective 3 corresponds only partly (a general compliance-cost indicator plus ATAF's informal-sector guidance), and objective 5 rests on an IMF efficiency measure that I could not cite to a page. The introduction paragraph (drafted in Pass 1b, marked "DRAFT for owner review") therefore describes the five as the framework's design objectives and does not claim that they are widely recognised. To upgrade that wording the owner would need (a) the exact bibliographic details and page of the IMF/ISORA source and (b) a readable copy of the ATAF informal-sector guidebook or the OECD presumptive-regime paper, and the cited works would then have to be added to the report's reference list.
