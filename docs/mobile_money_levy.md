# Mobile-money levy in Tanzania: statutory basis, schedule, history, collections, and what the twin does with it

Written in Block F2 (2026-11-12). Documents are in `data/raw/` (git-ignored) and listed in `data/raw/SOURCES.csv`.

## 1. What the law is

* **Statutory basis.** Section 46A of the **National Payment Systems Act, 2015 (Cap. 437)**, inserted by the **Finance Act 2021** (Ministry of Finance and Planning, *Kauli ya Serikali*, 20 September 2022, p. 1: "Sheria ya Malipo ya Taifa, Sura na. 437 ... ilifanyiwa marekebisho kupitia Sheria ya Fedha ya mwaka 2021"). The regulations are made under s. 46A(2). **The Electronic and Postal Communications Act (EPOCA) is not the basis** of the mobile-money levy; its only link is the separate airtime / SIM "development levy" (s. 164A), which the Finance Act 2023 repealed (EY Tax Alert, 7 July 2023, p. 9).
* **Form.** A **tiered fixed fee per transaction** (TZS amount by value band), not a percentage. The legal incidence is on the **user**; the "collector" (a bank, financial institution or electronic money issuer, i.e. the mobile-money operator) collects it and remits it to TRA within seven days of the following month (GN 478V, regs 4-7).
* **On top of** VAT (18%) and 10% excise duty on mobile-money fees (GSMA 2023, p. 6; TRA Taxes and Duties at a Glance 2025/26, p. 29).

## 2. History, instrument by instrument

| Date in force | Instrument | What it did |
|---|---|---|
| 1 July 2021 (levy introduced; regulations in force from 15 July 2021) | National Payment Systems (Electronic Mobile Money Transfer and Withdrawal Transactions Levy) Regulations, 2021, **GN 496A of 2021** | 22-band schedule, TZS 10 to TZS 10,000 per transaction, transfers and withdrawals. Not retrieved (secondary citation: GSMA 2023, p. 5; Clyde & Co) |
| 1 September 2021 (GSMA, World Bank; the Minister's 2022 statement says 7 September) | **GN 642A of 2021** (Amendment Regulations, 31 August 2021) | Levy cut by about 30% (top band TZS 10,000 to 7,000). Not retrieved (secondary: GSMA 2023, p. 5) |
| 1 July 2022 | **GN 478V of 2022**, Electronic Money Transaction Levy Regulations, 2022 (Gazette Supplement 25, 1 July 2022) | Revokes GN 496A (reg. 13); top band TZS 4,000 (a 43% cut announced in the 14 June 2022 budget speech); base widened to bank-to-bank, bank-to-wallet and ATM / agent withdrawals. Retrieved: `data/raw/mof/GN_478V_...pdf`, schedule p. 7 |
| 1 October 2022 (Minister's statement p. 5; Gazette dated 5 October 2022) | **GN 596C of 2022**, Amendment Regulations (Gazette Supplement 39, 5 October 2022) | Replaces the whole Schedule: TZS 10 to TZS 2,000. Levy removed from bank-to-wallet, intra-bank and inter-bank transfers and from bank-agent and ATM withdrawals up to TZS 30,000. Retrieved: `data/raw/mof/GN_596C_...pdf`, schedule p. 2 |
| **1 July 2023** | **Finance Act 2023** (s. 48 per the ICTD factsheet; EY Tax Alert p. 9) | Amends s. 46A: the levy on electronic **transfers** is removed; it applies **only to cash withdrawals**. The Act's text was not retrieved (secondary: EY) |
| 2024, 2025, 2026 | Finance Acts 2024 and 2025 (full text searched), Finance Act 2026 (`data/raw/tra/`), the Finance Bill 2026 briefing | No amendment of the National Payment Systems Act or of the schedule found. The 2026/27 budget speech has no levy measure |
| 2025/26 | TRA, Taxes and Duties at a Glance 2025/26, p. 31 | "Mobile money transaction levy on withdrawing monies: From TZS 10 to TZS 2,000": the current schedule is still GN 596C |

(The 2023/24 budget speech proposed a 50% increase of the withdrawal levy; I found no evidence that it was gazetted: TRA still shows TZS 10 to 2,000 in 2025/26.)

## 4. Collections and an effective rate

* **No official collection total** was found for any fiscal year (TRA, MoF, BoT, budget documents opened by the search): the TRA monthly and annual collection reports and the budget performance reports were not opened.
* Published estimates: budget target 2021/22 TZS 1.254 trillion (Hansard, 23 June 2021, via Twaweza); about TZS 48.5 billion in the first four weeks (reported by the Minister, secondary); **TZS 360.45 billion for July-December 2021 and TZS 252 billion in calendar 2022** (GSMA estimates from operator data: Impact Analysis p. 12 and Impact Assessment 2023 p. 15). Proceeds spent in 2021/22 about TZS 358.8 billion (Minister's statement p. 2, a use-of-proceeds figure, not a collection total).
* **Effective rate.** Collections / BoT value of mobile payments for the same year exists only for **2022** (GSMA estimate TZS 252 billion; BoT NPS Annual Report value of mobile payments 2022 TZS 114,316 billion): **about 0.22%**, for the regime then in force (transfers and withdrawals; top band TZS 4,000 until 30 September, TZS 2,000 after). It is an industry estimate over a base wider than the levied transactions, so it understates the rate on levied value. **No effective rate can be derived for the current regime** (withdrawals only since July 2023): no collection figure exists for 2023, 2024 or 2025 and the BoT value does not separate withdrawals.

## 5. What the digital twin does with it

Nothing. The policy twin has **no mobile-money levy lever**: the owner removed it (Editing Pass 1, 2026-11-13: "not a framework lever"). The levy already exists, so its revenue is inside the observed tax-to-GDP baseline of the twin. The former lever (`twin.policy.mobile_money_levy`, then `twin.policy.hypothetical_additional_levy`, 0.3% of mobile-money payments, ASSUMPTION) is RETIRED in `config.yaml retired_assumptions`. This document is kept as the record of the statute, its history and the schedule.

## 6. The passage the data user quoted: citation

> "In June 2021, the average mobile money transaction fee for a transfer value of USD 18 in Tanzania was approximately USD 0.50 ... Tanzania introduced a mobile money transfer and withdrawal tax effective July 1, 2021. The levy was added on top of the VAT (18 percent) and fees (10 percent) (GMSA, 2021). Following the introduction of the levy, the average transaction fee for a transfer value of USD 18 increased to almost USD 1.20, a 140 percent increase. Responding to public concerns, the levy was reduced by about 30 percent, effective September 1, 2021. The average transaction fee for a transfer of USD 18 fell to about USD 1."

* **Paper:** Paul, Revocatus, and Dhiraj Sharma (2025). *The Impact of a Mobile Money Levy on Household Welfare: Evidence from Tanzania.* Policy Research Working Paper 11228. Washington, DC: World Bank, Poverty and Equity Global Department, October 2025. Introduction, PDF p. 4. https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099754010072536301/pdf/IDU-7b0add06-3ac9-4874-ab12-cdf10bd91449.pdf (read; not stored in `data/raw`).
* **Its GSMA reference:** reference [11], "GSMA. (2021). Tanzania mobile money levy impact analysis. GSMA Policy Report." (reference [12] is GSMA (2023), *Tanzania mobile money levy impact assessment*). The GSMA document is `data/raw/gsma/spec_tanzania_mm_report_02_22-1.pdf` (period 1 July-31 December 2021, copyright 2021, published February 2022) and the 2023 assessment is `data/raw/gsma/Tanzania-Mobile-Money-Levy-Impact-Assessment.pdf`. GSMA's own text (2023 report, p. 6) puts the taxes at 23% of the transfer cost until June 2021 (VAT 18% and excise duty 10% on fees) and 60% in July-August 2021. The "fees (10 percent)" of the World Bank paper is the 10% **excise duty on fees**, not a fee.
* **A different GSMA measure:** GSMA (2023, p. 7) reports that fees fell marginally, by 15% on average, in September 2021 (levy cut and operators' own fee cuts); the World Bank's "30 percent" is the cut in the **levy rate** (GN 642A). The two are not comparable.
